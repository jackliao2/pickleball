Teaching pickleball in PE

For PE teachers and coaches: the rules taught with a free game, a lesson plan, and a quiz. Updated 12 October 2026.

Quick answer: pair a short demonstration of the free VS Pickleball browser game with court time and our 25-question rules quiz. The game runs in any modern browser on Chromebooks, laptops or a projector, with no download and no student accounts, and it calls every fault by name, so students see the kitchen and two-bounce rules happen before they try them.

Pickleball is one of the easiest net sports to teach: the court is small, the paddles are short, and almost every student can rally in the first lesson. The hard part is the rules. The kitchen, the two-bounce rule and side-out scoring are abstract on a whiteboard, and students forget them the moment they pick up a paddle. Seeing them called in a game, then trying them on court, makes them stick.

What the game gives you

  • Every fault named. When a player volleys in the kitchen, hits before the second bounce, or serves into the kitchen, the game stops and says why.
  • Runs on school devices. It is a web page: no install, no student logins, no personal data entered. It works in Chrome on Chromebooks and on any recent laptop or tablet. (Your school’s network filter may still need to allow vspickleball.com.)

  • Difficulty levels so students who finish early can try hard mode.

A 45-minute lesson plan

TimeActivity
0–8 minProjector demo. Play a few points of the game in front of the class. Pause on each fault and ask students to name the rule. Deliberately volley from the kitchen once.
8–15 minRules on the board. Go through the five rules in the handout below, pointing to the court lines in the gym.
15–25 minDinking pairs. Students rally softly across the net from the kitchen line, letting the ball bounce in the kitchen. Count consecutive dinks.
25–40 minGames. Doubles or skinny singles to 7 with rally scoring (simpler than side-out for a first lesson). Students call faults themselves.
40–45 minExit check. Five questions from the rules quiz, aloud or on devices.

No gym time, or a wet day? Run the whole lesson in a computer lab: demo, then students play the game in pairs on 2 player mode, then take the full quiz.

Rules handout (copy for your students)

  1. Court: 20 × 44 ft. The kitchen is the 7 ft zone on each side of the net.
  2. Serve: underhand, from behind the baseline, diagonally into the opposite service court. It must clear the kitchen; the kitchen line is out on a serve.
  3. Two-bounce rule: the serve must bounce, and the return must bounce. After that, volleys are allowed.
  4. Kitchen: no volleys while touching the kitchen or its line. You can step in to hit a ball that bounced.
  5. Scoring: games to 11, win by 2. In traditional scoring only the serving team scores; in rally scoring every rally scores.

Full explanations for each rule: kitchen, two-bounce, serving, scoring, doubles.

Assessment

The pickleball rules quiz has 25 true-or-false and multiple-choice questions covering the court, serve, two-bounce rule, kitchen, scoring and doubles. Students tap an answer and immediately see the correct one with an explanation, so it works as practice as well as a check. For a graded test, read questions aloud and have students write answers.

Tips from the court

  • Start with rally scoring. Teach side-out scoring and the 0–0–2 start once students can rally.
  • Lower nets or foam balls help younger students; the rules stay the same.
  • Make the kitchen visible. Tape or cones on the kitchen line make the most important rule easy to see.
  • Mix levels with games. Around the world and king of the court keep big classes moving on few courts.

Common questions

Is there a free pickleball game students can play at school?
Yes. VS Pickleball runs in a web browser with no download and no accounts, on Chromebooks, laptops and tablets. Schools may need to allow the site through their network filter.
How do you teach pickleball rules to students?
Show the rules happening, then let students try them: a short game demo where each fault is named, a five-rule handout, dinking practice, short games with rally scoring, and a quick quiz to check understanding.
Should beginners learn rally scoring or side-out scoring?
Rally scoring is easier for a first lesson because every rally scores. Introduce side-out scoring and the 0-0-2 doubles call once students are comfortable rallying.
