Indoor vs outdoor pickleball

Same rules, different ball, different game. Updated 4 October 2026.

Quick answer: the rules are identical, but the balls are not. Outdoor balls are harder and slightly heavier with about 40 small holes, so they fly straighter in wind and play faster. Indoor balls are softer and lighter with about 26 larger holes, so they play slower and are easier to control on a gym floor. Use the ball made for your surface: an outdoor ball skids on wood, and an indoor ball gets blown around and wears out quickly outside.

Walk from a community-centre gym to a park court and you will notice it within three shots: the ball sounds different, bounces differently, and your dinks that floated nicely inside suddenly sit up or die in the net. Nothing about the rules has changed. Everything about the ball and the surface has.

Every approved ball, indoor or outdoor, has to fall within the same official limits for size, weight and bounce, which is why the rules do not change. The differences are in how the plastic and the hole pattern behave in the air and off the surface. Outdoor balls such as the Franklin X-40 and Dura Fast 40 are the common tournament balls outside; indoor balls are designed with fewer, larger holes for the slower, grippier gym game. Most groups have a house ball, so ask before you buy a tube.

The courts

Indoors you are usually on a wooden or sport-tile gym floor, with pickleball lines taped or painted over basketball and badminton markings. That is the first thing to get used to: lots of extra lines, and the pickleball ones are the ones that matter. Floors are consistent, there is no wind, and the light is even, but ceilings can be low enough to limit lobs and the bounce can be lower on a slick floor. See court dimensions for the lines you need to find.

Outdoors you are usually on an acrylic-coated hard court, either a dedicated pickleball court or lines added to a tennis court. The surface is grittier, the ball bounces higher and faster, and you deal with wind, sun and temperature. Cold makes outdoor balls harder and more likely to crack; heat makes them a little livelier.

How strategy changes

  • Outdoors favours power a little more. The harder ball moves faster, so drives and speed-ups are more effective and hands battles at the line happen quicker. The banger is more dangerous outside.
  • Indoors favours touch. The softer ball is easier to keep low, so dinking rallies last longer and the reset is easier to pull off. Patience wins more points.
  • Wind changes your targets. Hitting into the wind, you can swing more freely; with the wind behind you, take pace off and aim deeper in the court than you think. Lobs are risky outside on a windy day.
  • Serve and return depth matters everywhere, but a deep serve with the outdoor ball skids through and is harder to return deep.

Switching between indoor and outdoor

  • Give it ten minutes of dinking before playing games, to recalibrate how hard to push the ball.
  • Moving outside: expect the ball to come faster and bounce higher; shorten your backswing and get your paddle up earlier.
  • Moving inside: expect a slower, lower ball; move your feet to get under it, and be more patient before attacking.
  • Shoes: indoor court shoes with non-marking gum soles for gyms; tennis shoes with tougher soles for outdoor hard courts.

Common questions

What is the difference between indoor and outdoor pickleballs?
Outdoor balls are harder and slightly heavier with about 40 small holes, so they play faster and hold their line in wind. Indoor balls are softer and lighter with about 26 larger holes, so they play slower and are easier to control on gym floors.
Can you use an outdoor pickleball indoors?
You can, and some indoor groups do, but the harder ball skids on wood floors and plays faster than most indoor players expect. Check what ball your group uses.
Can you use an indoor pickleball outdoors?
It is not recommended. The lighter ball with large holes is blown around by wind and wears out quickly on gritty hard courts.
Are pickleball rules different indoors and outdoors?
No. The rules, court size and net height are the same. Only the ball and the playing surface differ.
Why do outdoor pickleballs crack?
The hard plastic becomes more brittle in cold weather and takes repeated hard hits on a rough surface. Outdoor balls are a consumable; replace them when they crack or go out of round.
