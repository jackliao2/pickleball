Pickleball court dimensions

Every measurement on the court, what each line is for, and how much room you need around it. Updated 21 September 2026.

A pickleball court is small enough to fit in a two-car driveway and precise enough that a line an inch out of place changes how the game plays. This page has every number, from the 44-foot length to the 34-inch net sag, and explains what each line does during a rally. If you are marking out a court in a gym or on a tennis court, or just trying to picture why the far player in a game seems so close, start here.

All measurements are taken to the outside edge of the lines. The 2-inch lines are inside the 20 × 44 foot rectangle, not added to it. The same court is used for singles and doubles, unlike tennis and badminton, which widen for doubles.

What each line does

Baselines

The short lines at each end, 44 feet apart. You serve from behind your baseline: both feet behind it at contact, per the serving rules. A ball landing on the baseline is in.

Sidelines

The long lines, 20 feet apart. A ball on the sideline is in. The sidelines also define the width of the service box for foot placement: the server has to be within an imaginary extension of the sideline and centreline.

Non-volley zone line (kitchen line)

Seven feet from the net on each side, spanning the full width. It is the most important line on the court and the only one that behaves differently depending on the shot. For your feet, it belongs to the kitchen: touch it while volleying and it is a fault. For a serve, it is out: a serve landing on it is short. For any other shot, a ball landing on it is in. The kitchen rule guide covers all of it.

Centreline

Runs from each baseline to the kitchen line, splitting each back court into a left and right service box. It does not continue through the kitchen; the kitchen is one undivided strip. A serve on the centreline is in.

Service boxes

Each is 10 feet wide and 15 feet deep, bounded by the baseline, a sideline, the centreline and the kitchen line. The serve must land in the box diagonally opposite the server, and the server stands behind the box on the right at even scores and on the left at odd, as explained in the scoring guide.

The net

The net is a little wider than the court: 22 feet between posts, so the posts stand a foot outside each sideline. It measures 36 inches high at the sidelines and sags to 34 inches at the centre, held down by a centre strap. Two inches of sag is enough to make the middle the highest-percentage place to hit over, which is why “down the middle solves the riddle” is a coaching cliché. A tennis net is 42 inches at the posts and 36 at the centre, so a tennis net lowered on its strap is still too high at the posts; proper pickleball play needs a dedicated net or a portable one.

Room around the court

The 20 × 44 rectangle is only the lines. You need space beyond them to chase lobs, take an Erne, and stop safely. USA Pickleball recommends a minimum overall area of 30 by 60 feet, which gives 5 feet beyond each sideline and 8 feet behind each baseline. For tournaments the preferred footprint is 34 by 64 feet. On a converted tennis court these clearances take care of themselves; in a gym or a driveway they are what decides whether the court is actually playable.

Fitting pickleball onto other courts

A tennis court measures 36 × 78 feet inside the doubles lines, with a fenced area usually around 60 × 120. That is enough for one pickleball court across the middle with generous run-off, two side by side, or four with blended lines and temporary nets, which is how most clubs have been converting. A doubles badminton court is exactly 20 × 44 feet, identical to a pickleball court, which is not a coincidence: the sport was invented in 1965 on a badminton court, and the net was simply lowered. The comparison guide has all four courts drawn to the same scale.

Marking out a temporary court

  1. Mark the net line first, then measure 22 feet along it from one post position to the other, with the net centred on the court.
  2. From the centre of the net line, measure 7 feet in each direction for the kitchen lines, then 22 feet in each direction for the baselines.
  3. Measure 10 feet either side of the centre for the sidelines, giving the 20-foot width.
  4. Check the diagonals. A 20 × 44 rectangle has diagonals of 48.33 feet (48 ft 4 in). If both diagonals match, the corners are square.
  5. Add the centreline from each baseline to each kitchen line at the 10-foot mark.

Painter’s tape works on a gym floor; chalk or throw-down lines work outdoors. Lines should contrast with the surface, and the kitchen line in particular needs to be visible from the baseline, because it is the line players argue about.

Common questions

How big is a pickleball court?
20 by 44 feet inside the lines (6.10 × 13.41 m), for both singles and doubles. Allow at least 30 by 60 feet overall.
How high is the net?
36 inches at the posts, 34 inches at the centre.
Is the kitchen 7 feet from the net or from the baseline?
From the net. It is the 7-foot strip on each side of the net, leaving 15 feet of back court behind it.
How wide are the lines?
2 inches, and they sit inside the court measurements.
Is a pickleball court the same size as a badminton court?
Yes, exactly the size of a doubles badminton court, 20 × 44 feet.
How many pickleball courts fit on a tennis court?
One with lots of room, two comfortably, or four with blended lines and portable nets.
