How to set up a backyard pickleball court

From a full permanent court to chalk lines on a driveway. Updated 4 October 2026.

Quick answer: the playing area is 20 × 44 ft, but you want about 30 × 60 ft of flat, hard surface so players can move behind and beside the lines (34 × 64 ft is ideal). Concrete or asphalt with an acrylic sport coating is the standard surface. A portable 22 ft net and taped or painted lines get you playing; a driveway with a shorter, narrower court works fine for practice.

A home court is the dream of every pickleball player who has waited forty minutes for a game at the park. The good news is that pickleball needs far less space than tennis: you can fit roughly four pickleball courts on one tennis court. The less good news is that the space around the lines matters as much as the lines themselves, and that pickleball is loud. Plan for both.

The extra space is not a luxury. Deep serves and lobs take players several feet behind the baseline, and wide angles pull them off the sidelines. A court with a fence or wall right on the line is a twisted ankle waiting to happen. Every line measurement, the kitchen, the service courts and the net, is in pickleball court dimensions.

Surface

  • Concrete is the most durable base and the most common for permanent courts. It needs proper slope for drainage and expansion joints placed to avoid the playing lines where possible.
  • Asphalt is cheaper to lay but can crack over time, especially in climates with freeze and thaw.
  • Acrylic sport coating on top of either gives the textured, consistent surface you find on public courts, plus colour and painted lines.
  • Interlocking sport tiles can go over an existing flat slab and drain well; they change the bounce slightly.
  • Grass, gravel and pavers do not work: the ball needs a hard, even surface to bounce predictably.

For a permanent court, get quotes from a sports-surface contractor; local costs vary widely with size, base and region, and permits or HOA approval may be needed.

Net and lines

  • Portable nets are 22 ft wide, set up in minutes and are the usual choice for driveways and multi-use slabs. Check that the centre can be set to 34 inches.
  • Permanent posts set in the ground give a tighter, more consistent net and stand up to wind, but commit you to the layout.
  • Lines are 2 inches wide. For temporary courts use court tape, rubber throw-down lines or chalk; for permanent courts, painted acrylic lines. Remember the lines are part of the area they mark: a ball on the line is in.

Driveway and small-space courts

Most driveways are not 30 × 60 ft, and that is fine. A shorter, narrower court still gives you the most important part of pickleball: the kitchen game. Mark two kitchen lines 7 ft either side of a portable net and you have a perfect dinking and volley practice space. Add whatever back court you can fit for serves. Many players get more improvement from twenty minutes of driveway dinking a day than from one long weekly session. Noise and neighbours

The pop of a hard paddle on a hard ball carries further than people expect, and pickleball noise has become a real source of neighbourhood disputes. Before building, check local noise rules and HOA covenants, think about where the court sits relative to neighbours’ windows, and talk to them early. Quieter balls and paddles exist and help, fencing with acoustic panels helps more, and reasonable playing hours help most.

Lighting, fencing and extras

  • Fencing 8 to 10 ft high at each end saves a lot of ball chasing; lower fencing along the sides is usually enough.
  • Lighting should be even and placed beside the court, not at the ends, so players do not look into it on lobs.
  • Orientation: a court running roughly north–south keeps the low sun out of players’ eyes in the morning and evening.
  • Shade and seating near the gate for players waiting their turn.

Common questions

How much space do you need for a backyard pickleball court?
The playing area is 20 × 44 ft, but about 30 × 60 ft is the recommended minimum so players can move beyond the lines, and 34 × 64 ft is ideal.
Can you play pickleball in a driveway?
Yes. A portable net and taped or chalked lines turn a driveway into a practice court. Even a short, narrow court is ideal for dinking and kitchen drills.
What is the best surface for a backyard pickleball court?
Concrete or asphalt with an acrylic sport coating is the standard. Interlocking sport tiles over a flat slab also work. Grass and gravel do not, because the ball needs a hard, even bounce.
How wide is a pickleball net?
A standard portable pickleball net is 22 ft wide, set at 36 inches at the sidelines and 34 inches in the centre.
Is pickleball noisy for neighbours?
It can be. The hard paddle and ball make a sharp pop that carries. Check local rules and HOA covenants, consider quieter balls and paddles, add acoustic fencing, and keep reasonable hours.
