How to beat bangers

Every open-play session has one: the player who hits every ball as hard as they can. Here is how to stop losing to them. Updated 27 September 2026.

A banger is a player who drives nearly every ball as hard as possible, usually flat and usually at you. At 2.5 and 3.0 they win a lot, because most people at that level cannot handle pace and panic into swinging back. At 3.5 and above they win much less, because players there have learned the handful of habits below. None of them require hitting harder. The whole point is that you do not need to.

Why bangers win at first

Pace creates errors in three ways. It gives you less time to prepare, so you hit late. It makes you tense, so your grip tightens and the ball flies. And it tempts you to swing back just as hard, which is the lowest-percentage shot available. A banger does not need to hit winners; they need you to make one of those three mistakes before they miss. Beating them is about removing those mistakes, not out-hitting them.

1. Get your paddle up and out in front

This is the single biggest fix. At the kitchen line, hold the paddle in front of your chest, face slightly open, elbow relaxed, before every ball. Against a hard drive you do not have time to bring the paddle up from your knees. With it already up, most drives at your body are simply blocked because the paddle is in the way. A lot of the “pace” problem is really a ready-position problem.

2. Block, do not swing

When a hard ball comes at you, the answer is a block: firm wrist, loose grip, no backswing, paddle face angled so the ball rebounds softly. You are using their pace, not adding your own. A good block takes the speed off the ball and lands it short, ideally in the kitchen, which forces the banger to hit up. Swinging at a hard ball returns it harder and higher, and the banger lives on high balls.

Grip pressure matters more than technique here. On a scale of 1 to 10, hold the paddle at about a 3 when blocking. Tight grips send the ball long; loose ones absorb it.

3. Let the out balls go

Bangers hit a lot of balls out. A flat drive at shoulder height or above, still rising as it reaches you at the kitchen line, is almost always going to land beyond the baseline. The instinct is to hit it because it is coming at you. Don’t. Move your paddle and your head out of the way, turn sideways if you must, and watch it land long. Every out ball you let go is a free point; every one you hit is a chance for you to make the error instead.

A workable rule: at the line, if a drive is above your chest and rising, let it go. If it is at your waist or below, block it. The exception is heavy topspin, which dips late; you learn to spot it after a few sessions against the same player.

4. Get to the line

Bangers are most dangerous when you are in the transition zone or at the baseline, because their drives land at your feet and you have to hit up. From the kitchen line, the same drive has to travel over the net and arrive at chest height, where you can block it. The first rule of beginner strategy applies twice over: get to the line and stay there.

5. Serve and return deep

A banger wants a short ball they can step into. Deep serves and deep returns make them hit from the baseline, where a drive has to be lifted over the net and is much easier to block. The serve and return guide covers depth in detail.

6. Drop the third, even when they are at the net

When you are serving, the banger will be at the kitchen line waiting for your third ball. If you drive it, you are playing their game at their range. A soft third-shot drop into the kitchen forces them to hit a low ball up. Bangers hate hitting up, because a low ball cannot be banged; they have to be patient or drive it into the net. Many do the latter.

7. Keep the ball low and make them dink

This is how you actually win the rallies. A banger can only bang a ball that is above the net. Keep every shot low, dinks into the kitchen, drops at their feet, and they have nothing to hit hard. They will either try to drive a low ball, which usually goes into the net or rises to a blockable height, or they will dink, which is not their game. The dinking guide covers keeping the ball down.

8. Aim at their feet and their backhand

Bangers generate pace with a big forehand. Low balls to the feet and balls to the backhand take that away. A banger reaching for a low backhand either pushes it softly, which you can attack, or swings anyway and misses.

9. Stay calm and count

Keep a mental count of how many points the banger wins with winners and how many they lose with errors. Against a disciplined blocker, most bangers lose more points than they win. Knowing that makes it much easier not to panic and start swinging back.

Doubles against a banger

  • Hit to the partner. The banger’s partner often hits softer and more consistently. Keep the ball away from the banger’s forehand.
  • Crosscourt, low. A low crosscourt dink to the banger means they are hitting from their sideline over the high part of the net.
  • Stay level with your partner. A gap between you is where the drive will go.

What not to do

  • Try to out-bang them. They practise it; you don’t.
  • Lob from the baseline. Bangers are usually good overhead hitters.
  • Back up. Moving back gives them more court to hit into and more time for their ball to dip.
  • Complain about it. Hitting hard is legal; so is aiming at your body. The answer is paddle up.

Common questions

What is a banger in pickleball?
A player who drives nearly every ball as hard as they can, usually flat and often at the opponent’s body, instead of dinking or dropping.
How do you return a hard drive at the kitchen line?
Block it: paddle already up in front, loose grip, no backswing, face slightly open. The goal is to take the pace off and land it softly in the kitchen.
Should I let hard balls go?
At the kitchen line, a flat drive above your chest that is still rising is usually going out. Get out of the way and let it land long.
Is it legal to hit the ball at an opponent?
Yes. Hitting a player with the ball before it bounces wins the rally. It is part of the game, which is why the paddle-up ready position matters.
How do I stop getting hit by the ball?
Keep your paddle in front of your chest between shots and get to the kitchen line, where the ball arrives at a height you can block.

Keep going: the reset is the defensive shot that turns a banger’s pace into a soft ball, and dinking is what keeps the ball out of their strike zone.
