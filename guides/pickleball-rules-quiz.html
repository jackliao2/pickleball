Pickleball rules quiz

25 questions, answers explained, no sign-up. Tap an answer to check it. Updated 12 October 2026.

How well do you really know the rules? This quiz covers what comes up on court and on PE tests: the court, the serve, the two-bounce rule, the kitchen, scoring and doubles. Tap an answer to see whether you are right and why. Every answer links to the guide that explains it properly.

  1. A rally ends when one team faults. True or false?

    Answer: True. A fault, such as hitting into the net, hitting out, volleying from the kitchen, or letting the ball bounce twice, ends the rally immediately. Learn more.

  2. How wide and long is a pickleball court?

    Answer: 20 × 44 ft. A pickleball court is 20 ft wide and 44 ft long, the same size as a doubles badminton court. Learn more.

  3. How deep is the kitchen (non-volley zone) on each side of the net?

    Answer: 7 ft. The kitchen extends 7 ft from the net on each side. Learn more.

  4. How high is the net at the centre of the court?

    Answer: 34 in. The net is 34 inches at the centre and 36 inches at the sidelines. Learn more.

  5. The serve must be hit underhand. True or false?

    Answer: True. For a volley serve, the paddle moves upward and contact is made below the waist. The drop serve, where the ball bounces first, is also legal. Learn more.

  6. Where must the serve land?

    Answer: In the diagonally opposite service court. The serve goes diagonally, crosscourt, into the opposite service court, beyond the kitchen line. Learn more.

  7. A serve that lands on the kitchen line is…

    Answer: Out (a fault). A fault. The kitchen line counts as part of the kitchen, so a serve landing on it is short. Learn more.

  8. A serve that lands on the baseline is…

    Answer: In. Except for the kitchen line on a serve, a ball landing on a line is in. Learn more.

  9. What does the two-bounce rule say?

    Answer: The serve and the return must each bounce before being hit. The serve must bounce before it is returned, and the return must bounce before the serving side hits it. After that, volleys are allowed. Learn more.

  10. After the serve and the return have bounced, may players volley?

    Answer: Yes, from outside the kitchen. Yes. Once both bounces have happened, either team may volley, as long as they are not in the kitchen. Learn more.

  11. Can you stand in the kitchen?

    Answer: Yes, any time. Yes. You can stand in the kitchen and hit a ball that has bounced. What you cannot do is volley while touching the kitchen. Learn more.

  12. You volley from just behind the kitchen line and your momentum carries you into the kitchen. Is it a fault?

    Answer: Yes. If momentum from a volley carries you into the kitchen, even after the ball is dead, it is a fault. Learn more.

  13. Your toe touches the kitchen line while you volley. Is it a fault?

    Answer: Yes. The line is part of the kitchen, so touching it while volleying is a fault. Learn more.

  14. What is a dink?

    Answer: A soft shot that lands in the opponent’s kitchen. A dink is a soft shot, usually from near the kitchen line, that drops into the opponent’s kitchen. Learn more.

  15. In traditional (side-out) scoring, which team can score a point?

    Answer: Only the serving team scores in side-out scoring. The receiving team wins the right to serve. Learn more.

  16. A standard pickleball game is played to…

    Answer: 11, win by 2. Games are usually to 11, win by 2. Some tournament formats use 15 or 21. Learn more.

  17. In rally scoring, a point is scored…

    Answer: On every rally, by whoever wins it. In rally scoring every rally produces a point for the winner, whoever served. Learn more.

  18. In doubles, what does the score call “4–2–1” mean?

    Answer: Serving team 4, receivers 2, first server. Serving team’s score first, then the receiving team’s score, then whether this is server 1 or server 2. Learn more.

  19. Why does a doubles game start at 0–0–2?

    Answer: The first serving team gets only one server. To offset the advantage of serving first, the first team gets only one server in its first turn, so the call treats them as already on server 2. Learn more.

  20. In doubles, when the serving team wins a point, what happens?

    Answer: The server switches sides with their partner. The server and partner swap sides, and the server serves again from the other side. Learn more.

  21. The ball bounces twice on your side before you hit it. What happens?

    Answer: It is a fault against you. Two bounces on one side is a fault against that side. Learn more.

  22. A serve clips the net and lands in the correct service court. What happens?

    Answer: It is in and play continues. Under current rules there are no service lets: a serve that touches the net and lands in the correct court is in, and play continues. Learn more.

  23. Is a ball that hits your body before bouncing a fault against you?

    Answer: Yes. If the ball touches you or your clothing before it bounces, you lose the rally, even if it was going out. Learn more.

  24. What is the third-shot drop?

    Answer: A soft shot by the serving team on the third shot, aimed into the kitchen. Because of the two-bounce rule, the serving team is at the back on the third shot. A soft drop into the kitchen lets them move up to the net. Learn more.

  25. Is stacking legal in doubles?

    Answer: Yes. Teams may arrange where partners stand after the serve and return, as long as the correct player serves and receives from the correct side. Learn more.

Common questions

A rally ends when one team faults: true or false?
True. Any fault, such as hitting into the net, hitting out, volleying from the kitchen or a double bounce, ends the rally immediately.
What are the most commonly tested pickleball rules?
The court size (20 × 44 ft), the 7 ft kitchen, the underhand diagonal serve, the two-bounce rule, no volleying in the kitchen, and scoring to 11, win by 2, with only the serving team scoring in traditional scoring.
Is a serve that hits the net and lands in replayed?
No. Under current rules there are no service lets; a serve that touches the net and lands in the correct service court is in and play continues.
Can you volley in the kitchen?
No. You may not volley while touching the kitchen or its line, and you may not let your momentum carry you into it after a volley. You may enter the kitchen to hit a ball that has bounced.
